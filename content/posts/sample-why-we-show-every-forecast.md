**Sample draft.** This is a placeholder post for the prototype. It hasn’t been reviewed, and it doesn’t describe any real forecasts.

Ask when whole brain emulation might arrive and you can get very different answers. One forecaster might give a window a few decades out. Another might doubt it will ever be practical. If we averaged answers like those into a single year, we’d be claiming a precision nobody has.

So the timeline draws every forecast as its own line, with who made it, when they made it, the window they gave and the exact quote. Next to them is a count, like “4 forecasts from 3 sources”, that shows whether a milestone has been forecast many times or only once.

## When we do draw a range

If the team has set a range, it shows as a solid bar, and you can read the written basis for it. If there’s no team range and the forecasts roughly agree, we shade the span they cover. If they don’t agree, there’s no shading, just the individual forecasts and a “Sources disagree” stamp.

The exact test for when credible sources “disagree” substantially is on the [methodology page](/methodology/#timeline), along with how the team can override it and why an override has to be published.

## What this costs

A single date would be easier to read at a glance. The timeline asks a bit more of you, with several lines where there could be one and a stamp wherever the forecasters split. We think the extra reading is worth it, but if you find a clearer way to show the same uncertainty, [we’d like to hear it](/contact/).
