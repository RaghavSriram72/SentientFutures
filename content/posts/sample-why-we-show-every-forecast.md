**Sample draft.** This is a placeholder post for the prototype. It hasn’t been reviewed, and it doesn’t describe any real forecasts.

Ask when whole brain emulation might arrive and you can get very different answers. One forecaster might give a window a few decades out. Another might say it may never be practical at all. If we averaged answers like those into a single year, we’d be showing a precision that nobody actually has.

So the timeline draws every forecast as its own line: who made it, when they made it, the window they gave and the exact quote. Next to them you’ll see a count, like “4 forecasts from 3 sources”, so you can tell a well-studied milestone from one that rests on a single guess.

## When we do draw a range

Sometimes there is a range. If the team has set one, it shows as a solid bar, with a written basis you can read. If there’s no team range and the forecasts roughly agree, we shade the span they cover. When they don’t agree, we shade nothing. You get the individual forecasts and a “Sources disagree” stamp instead.

> Conflicting forecasts are never collapsed into one precise date. When credible sources disagree substantially, the site shows the disagreement.

That’s the rule we hold ourselves to. The exact test we use for “disagree” is on the [methodology page](/methodology/#timeline), along with how the team can override it and why that override has to be published.

## What this costs

A single date would be easier to read at a glance. The timeline asks a little more of you: a few lines instead of one, and a stamp where the experts split. We think that’s the honest trade. If you find a clearer way to show the same uncertainty, [we’d like to hear it](/contact/).
